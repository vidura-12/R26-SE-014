## CINNAMON SOIL AND CLIMATE REQUIREMENTS

Cinnamomum zeylanicum trees should be grown on deep, well-drained moist soils. The soil texture must be a mix of sand, gravel, clay and silt particles to be suitable for Ceylon Cinnamon cultivation, however none of them represent a higher share than 50%. The soil on which the cinnamon plants is grown varies mainly from red-yellow podzolic soil to reddish brown latosol.

The cultivation of Ceylon Cinnamon can be found in altitudes of 10 m to 700 m above sea level. Cinnamomum zeylanicum need a lot of water and high humidity level to grow properly. Regions with 1250 mm/yr. to 3500 mm/yr. precipitation are perfectly suited for their cultivation and can be grown in regions where irrigation is sometimes required.

In Sri Lanka, Cinnamon seems to have originated in the central hills where seven wild species of cinnamon occur in Kandy, Matale, Belihull oya, Haputale, Horton planes and the Sinharaja forest range. Presently cultivation concentrated along the coastal belt from Negambo to Matara, it has also made inroads to Kalutara and Ratnapura.

The ideal temperature for growing cinnamon is between 20°C and 30°C. Rainfall should be in the range of 1250-2500 mm. Generally, cinnamon does not thrive well in the drier parts of the low-country. It thrives well as a forest tree at 300-350 m above sea level.

The quality of bark is greatly influenced by soil and ecological factors. The best quality cinnamon is produced on white silicatious sandy soils like the silver sands of the Negombo district. Yield is higher in other soils but the quality is coarser than in sandy soils.

## SOIL PH AND FERTILITY MANAGEMENT

The ideal pH for Cinnamomum zeylanicum production is between 5.5 to 6.5. Usage of fertilizer should be according to recommendation of Department of Export agriculture and with refer to soil nutrient analysis test report.

If the pH is too low (it sometimes drops because of the leaves and other organic materials left on the field), dolomite should be applied (1000kg/ha/yr) to rise the pH level.

It is recommended to maintain or improve soil fertility based on soil analysis report (On N, P, K Analysis) or diagnosed common plant nutrient deficiency and toxicity symptoms.

The hole in which the plant will be grown need to be filled with topsoil.

---

## PLANTING AND SPACING RECOMMENDATIONS

The trees are grown as bushes, consisting of 3 to 15 seeds or seedlings planted close to each other, this is called a pandura in the local language. The bushes are cultivated at a spacing of 4 feet between rows by 3 feet within rows. The rows are arranged in a parallel line pattern while the land is flat. If the land is on a slope, the rows are arranged in a zig-zag pattern organized in contour lines in order to avoid soil erosion.

On flat land cinnamon can be planted in straight lines and on sloping land planting on contour lines is recommended. Holes of 30cm x 30cm x 30cm for planting are dug at 1-2m x 0.6m spacing and filled with top soil. At this spacing 14,000 holes can be dug in one hectare. Planting must be done during the onset of rain, at a rate of three plants per hole. A single hectare plantation requires 42,000 plantlets.

---

## SOIL CONSERVATION AND DRAINAGE

When the land is sloppy the following soil conservation measures should be adopted: Digging of contour drains at suitable distances depending on the slope and the rainfall. Drains should be deep enough to allow for the settlement of eroded soil.

Mulching with pruned branches and weeds is recommended. Burying weeds and pruned leaves around rootstocks is helpful for better stooling. Gathering earth up to the rootstock without mounting should be done as cinnamon is a surface feeder. Rootstocks should be exposed to sunlight to allow new shoots to develop and to prevent termite attack.

## FERTILIZER APPLICATION SCHEDULE

Fertilizer application is important for the commercial cultivation of cinnamon for higher productivity, as cinnamon is normally grown as a long-term monoculture. In the absence of fertilizer application the supply of nutrients in the soil will become exhausted, leading to mineral deficiencies and a drop in yield, which includes dry matter yield as well as the oil content of bark and leaves and also poor stooling.

The Department of Export Agriculture of Sri Lanka recommends fertilizer mixture with Urea 2 parts and Rock phosphate 1 part.

The fertilizer requirement will vary according to the age of the plantation. Following are the recommended rates of fertilizer for young plantations:
First year: 200 kg/ha/annum
Second year: 400 kg/ha/annum
Third year: 600 kg/ha/annum

The above quantities have to be applied as two splits at six month intervals. It is important to apply fertilizer when the soil is moist or at the commencement of rains. Fertilizer should be applied at a 50 cm radius around the plant or between rows. After application of the fertilizer it is important to fork it into soil.

In addition, when there are symptoms of magnesium deficiency the application of Dolomite at the rate of 500 kg/ha two to three months before applying the recommended fertilizer mixture would be advantageous.

After three years when the cinnamon is mature, the dose of fertilizer should be doubled for every successive application thereafter. However, the fertilizer requirement may be determined according to the yield potential.

## TRAINING AND PRUNING PRACTICES

The objective of training cinnamon plants is to establish a strong base which is capable of producing a greater number of healthy stems. When seedlings attain the age of about two years, and the basal diameter is about 4-6 cm, the main stem is coppiced or cut back to a height of about 4-6 cm from ground level. Cutting is done with a sharp knife having a long handle (keththa) at a 30 degree angle in such a way that the cut faces inwards. This will promote the tillering from the base towards the outside.

Only three strong and straight tillers are retained while all others should be removed so as to promote the growth of these shoots as the main stems. After one to two years, the main stems are harvested (pruned) alternately. It is necessary to allow each base of the pruned stem to initiate three healthy new stems. This practice generates five to eight stems from a single bush for harvesting per year after eight to ten years.

It is also important to remove side branches of the main stem to promote strong growth of stems. In addition, the pruning of side branches will expose the base of the plant to sunlight, which is believed to initiate more tillers from the base. It is also very important to make sure that all the harvesting cuts are made at an angle of 30 degrees inward as this encourages the clump to spread outwardly.

## DISEASE AND PEST MANAGEMENT GENERAL PRINCIPLES

Pests, diseases, and weeds must be controlled by a combination of the following measures: Mechanical cultivation by hand and/or machine. Application of chemical substances. Crop rotation, inter-cropping or mixed cropping. Selection of disease-resistant or tolerant species/varieties. Thermal processes. Protection of natural enemies of pests through provisions favorable to them such as hedges, nesting sites, release of predators.

It is suitable mix cropping with Coconut. Both crops management practices should be done adequately. Cinnamon should not be established in the land parcel where rubber was established within a few years back.

Used chemical for management of crop, weed, pest should be included in national list of allowed chemical substance list in Sri Lanka (Pesticides Recommended, Department of agriculture, Peradeniya 2019).

## WHITE ROOT ROT AND PHYTOPHTHORA CINNAMOMI

Phytophthora cinnamomi is one of the most important pathogens in the cultivation of cinnamon. P. cinnamomi is an important pathogen of horticulture, affecting 319 genera in 90 families, principally forest trees, and found in 56 countries of tropical and subtropical regions.

Phytophthora cinnamomi has been included in the list of the 10 most destructive oomycetes and as one of the 100 worst invasive species and natural enemies worldwide. It may affect 5000 plant species worldwide.

The geographic origin of P. cinnamomi is not clearly established. However, it was first isolated from striped cankers of cinnamon trees in Sumatra, Indonesia; this suggests that it is indigenous to regions of tropical and subtropical countries and has also spread to other regions of the world.

The oomycete P. cinnamomi has been reported to affect Cinnamomum burmannii in the East Indies and Indonesia, C. camphora in Indonesia, C. culilawan in China and Indonesia, C. micranthum in Taiwan, and C. sintok and C. verum in Indonesia.

## ENVIRONMENTAL CONDITIONS FAVORING ROOT ROT INFECTION

The humidity conditions of 90% and the temperature of 25 to 28 degrees Celsius favored the infection in the crown neck or base of the stem, inducing symptoms of canker. Control plants did not present symptoms under normal conditions.

In cinnamon plantations located in lowland Mexico, the dieback symptom began with top-down leaf chlorosis, then rot, depressed areas, inner bark necrosis, and root rot.

Cinnamon, although a hardy plant, is susceptible to a wide variety of diseases during its various stages of development. The prevailing climatic conditions in humid areas, which favor crop development, also allow soil pathogens to start their infection cycle in agroforestry systems.

Soil-borne fungal pathogens thrive in warm and humid environments, particularly in temperatures ranging from 25°C to 30°C. Excess soil moisture and poor drainage also promote fungal growth and increase disease risk.

Environmental conditions favoring root rot development include: Soil Moisture greater than 60-80% promotes fungal growth. Soil pH 5.0-6.5 creates suitable environment for root pathogens. Temperature 25-30°C provides optimal fungal growth. Humidity greater than 70% increases disease spread.

## SYMPTOMS OF WHITE ROOT ROT DISEASE

Initial symptoms of wilting and decaying were observed in cinnamon plants 30 days after infection. At this time, stem, crown, and root were analyzed, making a cross section, and the infection advanced progressively from the stem to the crown, and the base of the stem became brown with exudate.

Root rot and canker in cinnamon includes cracking of the bark, with bluish depressed necrosis. Discoloration of the roots was observed frequently, with roots turning brown in color. Black necrosis was evident in the roots. In the large roots connected to the collar, generally only the periderm was necrotic. In the smaller roots, the wood was also affected. Bark cankers occurred at the base of the main stem up to 30-50 cm.

At the beginning, disease symptoms are observed on one or two stems in the cinnamon bush. The symptoms include the leaves turning yellow and withering. Eventually, the entire cinnamon bush dies. When such a tree is uprooted and the root system is examined, white fungal threads tightly bound to the surface of the roots can be observed.

## PREVENTION AND MANAGEMENT STRATEGIES FOR WHITE ROOT ROT

Growers should implement management strategies to avoid introduction of Phytophthora cinnamomi into nurseries or new plantations that could be susceptible to this pathogen.

Proper drainage is critical. Digging of contour drains at suitable distances depending on the slope and the rainfall helps prevent waterlogging. Drains should be deep enough to allow for the settlement of eroded soil.

Mulching with pruned branches and weeds helps regulate soil moisture. Burying weeds and pruned leaves around rootstocks is helpful for better stooling. Gathering earth up to the rootstock without mounting should be done as cinnamon is a surface feeder.

Rootstocks should be exposed to sunlight to allow new shoots to develop and to prevent termite attack. Exposing the base to sunlight is believed to initiate more tillers and may reduce fungal growth.

When large-scale replanting of a plantation is contemplated, consider the possibility of distilling the root bark to produce camphor-rich root bark oil used in pharmaceutical preparations from old plants.

## COPPER FUNGICIDE AND CHEMICAL CONTROL

Spraying Bordeaux mixture or any other copper fungicide may be necessary to control leaf spot or leaf blight caused by Colletotrichum gloeosporiodes. While this targets leaf diseases, maintaining overall plant health through fungicide application can reduce plant stress and susceptibility to root diseases.

Used chemical for management of crop, weed, pest should be included in national list of allowed chemical substance list in Sri Lanka (Pesticides Recommended, Department of agriculture, Peradeniya 2019).

---

## REPLANTING AND PLANTATION RENEWAL

When cinnamon plants are about 40-50 years old, their regenerating ability gets reduced considerably, resulting in decreased yields. Hence replacement of old or low yielding plantations becomes necessary.

If large-scale replanting of a plantation is contemplated, then it becomes worthwhile to consider the possibility of distilling the root bark to produce the camphor-rich root bark oil used in pharmaceutical preparations.

---

## INTERCROPPING AND CROP ROTATION

It is suitable to mix crop cinnamon with Coconut. Both crops management practices should be done adequately. Cinnamon should not be established in the land parcel where rubber was established within a few years back.

Crop rotation, inter-cropping or mixed cropping are recommended methods for pest and disease control.

## NURSERY HYGIENE AND PLANTING MATERIAL SELECTION

Ripe seeds are collected from selected mother plants with desired characteristics such as: erect stem with smooth bark, vigorous growth, ease of peeling the stem bark, resistance towards pests and diseases, and desired chemical composition of the oil.

Cinnamon can be propagated from seeds and cuttings of young three-leaved shoots. However, propagation by seeds is easier and is the most common practice. The seed viability diminishes rapidly with time, and the viability is very low after five weeks. As such, it is important to use fresh seeds for germination.

## HARVESTING AND POST-HARVEST PRACTICES

Cinnamon is ready for harvesting after two to three years when the plant reaches a height of 1.5-2m with three to four shoots and the bark turns brown in colour. The main shoot is coppiced or cut back to a height of about 5cm from ground level. Two to three crops are taken annually depending upon the rainfall.

Normally the harvesting of mature sticks is done following the two rainy seasons when the new flush of leaves have hardened. At this time the bark peels off easily. Under good management conditions, harvesting could be done more than twice per year. Such a practice, coupled with the split application of fertilizer, can help to increase the yield.

The mature shoot is coppiced at a height of about 2.5 inches (6 cm) from the ground level. The coppicing is done inwardly at an angle of about 45 degrees in one sharp cut. The stems are not harvested all at the same time from the bush, but alternately between harvesting seasons. One or two mature stems per bush remain not coppiced.

## RISK-BASED ADVISORY ACTIONS

For Low Risk conditions where soil pH is 5.5 to 6.5, soil moisture is below 30%, and temperature is 20-25 degrees Celsius: Conditions are favorable. Continue regular monitoring and maintain good drainage. Apply fertilizer according to Department of Export Agriculture recommendations. Ensure proper spacing and pruning.

For Medium Risk conditions where soil pH is 5.0 to 5.5 or 6.5 to 7.0, soil moisture is 30-45%, and temperature is 23-28 degrees Celsius: Early warning signs detected. Improve field drainage to prevent waterlogging. Reduce irrigation frequency. Monitor plants closely for early symptoms such as leaf yellowing. Apply dolomite if pH is too low.

For High Risk conditions where soil pH is 4.5 to 5.5, soil moisture is 45-60%, and temperature is 25-30 degrees Celsius: Favorable conditions for White Root Rot detected. Apply preventive fungicide such as copper-based fungicide. Improve field drainage immediately to prevent waterlogging. Reduce irrigation and allow soil to dry. Increase plant spacing to improve air circulation. Inspect root zones for early signs of fungal growth.

For Critical Risk conditions where soil pH is below 4.5 or above 7.0, soil moisture is above 60%, and temperature is above 30 degrees Celsius: URGENT critical conditions for White Root Rot. Stop irrigation immediately. Ensure drainage by digging contour drains. Apply approved fungicide treatment. Consult an agricultural officer from the Department of Export Agriculture. Consider removing severely infected plants to prevent spread to neighboring bushes.

## WATER MANAGEMENT GUIDELINES

Cinnamomum zeylanicum need a lot of water and high humidity level to grow properly. Regions with 1250 mm/yr. to 3500 mm/yr. precipitation are perfectly suited. However, excess soil moisture and poor drainage promote fungal growth and increase disease risk.

When the land is sloppy, digging of contour drains at suitable distances depending on the slope and the rainfall is essential. Drains should be deep enough to allow for the settlement of eroded soil and prevent water accumulation around root zones.

---

## DEPARTMENT OF EXPORT AGRICULTURE RECOMMENDATIONS

The Department of Export Agriculture of Sri Lanka recommends maintaining ideal pH between 5.5 to 6.5 for Cinnamomum zeylanicum production. Fertilizer should be according to recommendation of Department of Export agriculture and with refer to soil nutrient analysis test report.

The Department recommends the following fertilizer mixture: Urea 2 parts, Rock phosphate 1 part. Application rates vary by plantation age.

The Department of Export Agriculture has identified 19 high quality, high yielding selections through screening of 210 different accessions.

---

## DISEASE MONITORING AND EARLY DETECTION

Since White Root Rot develops underground before visible symptoms appear, regular monitoring of environmental conditions is essential. Early detection is difficult because symptoms such as leaf yellowing, wilting, bark peeling, and tree decline appear only after significant root damage has already occurred.

Farmers should monitor soil moisture, soil temperature, and soil pH continuously using IoT sensors. When environmental parameters indicate disease-favorable conditions, preventive action should be taken before visible symptoms appear.
